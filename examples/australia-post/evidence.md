# Evidence Basis — Australia Post

## Observed from official public material

Australia Post describes itself as a Government Business Enterprise that is self-funded and carries both commercial and Community Service Obligations.

Its FY25 public material reports:

- more than 2.2 billion items processed;
- delivery to 12.8 million delivery points;
- 4,118 Post Offices, including 2,523 outside major cities;
- more than 1,500 parcel-locker banks at over 915 locations in the fast-facts source;
- substantial customer digital traffic;
- parcel/service and letter businesses;
- support for small business through MyPost Business;
- a national retail and delivery network;
- international deliveries;
- continued investment in people, fleet, technology and facilities.

The Customer Commitment and Service Charter describes multiple delivery/access forms including street, roadside, counter delivery, locked/private bags and Post Office boxes, and an obligation to make the basic letter service broadly accessible.

## What the evidence supports at capability level

The public material supports enduring business needs around:

- customer relationships and service access;
- products/services and pricing/offer management;
- lodgement/acceptance of items;
- item movement and network operations;
- delivery and collection;
- retail/service-point operations;
- address/delivery-point information;
- international movement;
- revenue and payment handling;
- workforce, supplier/partner and enterprise governance.

## Implementation evidence kept separate

The following are evidence about implementation, not automatically capabilities:

- Post Offices;
- Parcel Lockers;
- electric delivery vehicles/bikes;
- MyPost Business;
- specific transformation/investment programmes;
- named digital channels.

## Unknown from public evidence

Public material does not establish enough detail to confidently model:

- internal organisational ownership of capabilities;
- full application/data architecture;
- exact separation of parcel, letter and logistics planning processes;
- internal capability maturity;
- all partner/licence operating responsibilities;
- internal risk/control decomposition.

These are left out rather than filled with generic postal-industry assumptions.
