# Review — Northstar Equipment Hire

## Evidence review

The L1 model stays within the business responsibilities supported by the fictional brief. It avoids adding detailed industry capabilities that the scenario does not establish.

## Anti-pattern review

| First-draft label | Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Sydney Sales | location + team | replace with Customer Management |
| Online Booking Portal | application/channel | map later to relevant capabilities |
| Fleet Operations Team | organisational unit | replace with Fleet Management |
| Workshop | location/facility | replace with Maintenance Management |
| Invoice Processing | process step | place within Revenue Management |
| Digital Transformation | temporary programme | map as change initiative |
| Microsoft 365 | vendor product | map as technology |
| Equipment Delivery | process/activity | place within Fulfilment Management |
| Supplier Contracts | artefact/process | place within Supplier Management |
| ERP Replacement | temporary programme | map as change initiative |

## Granularity review

The ten L1 capabilities are enterprise-scale abilities. None is a single workflow step, system, team or site.

Selected L2 decompositions use four siblings each:

- Fleet Management: Planning, Acquisition, Disposition, Performance Management;
- Maintenance Management: Planning, Execution, Serviceability Management, Parts Management;
- Rental Agreement Management: Quotation, Contract, Extension, Closure Management.

The L2s are narrower enduring abilities within one L1. They are not individual tasks such as `Send Quote` or `Close Work Order`.

## Boundary questions

Potential overlap requiring business validation:

- Equipment Availability Management vs Fleet Management;
- Rental Agreement Management vs Revenue Management;
- Maintenance Parts Management vs Supplier Management.

These overlaps are resolved through definitions and explicit scope, not by pretending the labels are self-explanatory.

## Review outcome

**ACCEPT as a worked candidate model.**

It is intentionally illustrative rather than an equipment-hire reference taxonomy.
