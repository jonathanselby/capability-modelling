# Selected Capability Definitions — Sydney Water

These definitions are architectural proposals derived from public evidence.

## Water Supply Management

**Definition**  
The ability to plan, produce and distribute drinking water so required supply is available to customers with appropriate continuity and service performance.

**Purpose**  
Provide reliable drinking-water service while balancing demand, infrastructure and operating constraints.

**Included**
- water source interface management;
- water treatment management;
- water distribution management;
- water quality management.

**Excluded**
- facility identity;
- detailed treatment workflow;
- cross-enterprise asset lifecycle governance.

## Asset Management

**Definition**  
The ability to set asset strategy, govern asset lifecycles, manage asset performance and maintain authoritative asset information across physical infrastructure.

**Purpose**  
Keep long-lived infrastructure safe, serviceable and aligned to current and future service demand.

**Included**
- asset strategy management;
- asset lifecycle management;
- asset performance management;
- asset information management.

**Excluded**
- individual asset classes as capability identities;
- capital projects as capability identities;
- supplier governance outside asset-specific use.

## Water Quality Management

**Definition**  
The ability to monitor, assure and manage required drinking-water quality performance and respond to quality deviations.

**Purpose**  
Maintain safe, compliant and transparent drinking-water quality within Water Supply Management.

**Included**
- water quality monitoring management;
- water quality compliance management;
- water quality incident management.

**Excluded**
- the physical treatment facility as an organisational capability;
- general environmental management;
- customer billing or service management.
