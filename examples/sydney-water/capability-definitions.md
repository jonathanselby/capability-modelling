# Selected Capability Definitions — Sydney Water

These definitions are architectural proposals derived from public evidence.

## Water Supply Management

**Definition**  
The ability to plan, produce and distribute drinking water so required supply is available to customers with appropriate continuity and service performance.

**Purpose**  
Provide reliable drinking-water service while balancing demand, infrastructure and operating constraints.

**Included**
- supply planning;
- production/treatment management at capability level;
- distribution management;
- supply continuity management.

**Excluded**
- facility identity;
- detailed treatment workflow;
- water-quality governance, which is separately modelled.

## Network & Asset Management

**Definition**  
The ability to plan, maintain, renew and govern the performance of physical infrastructure used to deliver water-related services.

**Purpose**  
Keep long-lived infrastructure safe, serviceable and fit for current and future service demand.

**Included**
- asset planning;
- maintenance management;
- renewal management;
- asset performance management.

**Excluded**
- individual asset classes as capability identities;
- construction projects as capabilities;
- supplier governance outside asset-specific use.

## Water Quality Management

**Definition**  
The ability to establish, monitor, assure and report required water-quality performance and respond to quality deviations.

**Purpose**  
Maintain safe, compliant and transparent drinking-water quality.

**Included**
- quality standards/requirements;
- monitoring;
- quality incident management;
- assurance and reporting.

**Excluded**
- the physical treatment facility as an organisational capability;
- general environmental management;
- customer billing/service management.
