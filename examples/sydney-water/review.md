# Review — Sydney Water

## Evidence review

The candidate model stays close to publicly evidenced service categories and utility responsibilities.

It avoids claiming detailed internal operating decomposition not recoverable from public material.

## Anti-pattern corrections

- treatment plants, reservoirs, pumping stations and networks -> assets/facilities mapped to relevant capabilities;
- `WaterFix` -> service/programme mapped principally to Resource & Environment Management and Customer Management;
- `Future Ready` -> transformation initiative mapped to affected enterprise capabilities;
- major infrastructure projects -> change/investment mapped to Infrastructure Delivery Management and Asset Management;
- `Customer Billing` -> L2 capability within Customer Management;
- `Water Quality Testing` -> activity within Water Quality Management;
- `Supplier Tenders` -> process within Supplier Management.

## Granularity review

The 14 L1 capabilities are enterprise-scale abilities comparable to the Australia Post and Northstar L1 examples.

The selected L2s each decompose an L1 into enduring sub-capabilities. They stop before work instructions, treatment stages or maintenance tasks.

## Boundary questions

Needs internal validation:

- Water Supply Management vs Resource & Environment Management;
- service-specific asset ownership vs cross-enterprise Asset Management;
- Wastewater Management vs Recycled Water Management responsibilities within the current decomposition;
- Resource & Environment Management vs Stormwater Management responsibilities;
- Infrastructure Delivery Management vs Asset Management across the investment lifecycle.

## Review outcome

**ACCEPT as a public-source worked candidate with explicit unknowns.**

The model is intentionally not presented as Sydney Water's official or complete architecture.
